Summit – Ski Track & Plan

Privacy Policy

How Summit handles your ski data and what happens when you use connected features.

Location and ski history

Summit asks for location access when you use ski tracking. When you start a recording, it can use precise GPS and continue while your iPhone is locked or Summit is in the background. Summit uses location samples and timestamps to draw your route and calculate metrics such as speed, distance, and vertical descent. Recording can work without cell service.

Summit stores ski days, route points, and activity segments on your iPhone. It also stores trip plans, favorites, preferences, and import data used to create ski days. Summit has no separate sign-in, advertising, or third-party usage analytics in the app covered by this policy.

Maps, weather, and resort information

  • Apple Maps. Resort searches and lookups can send search terms and geographic regions to Apple. Apple Maps displays maps and generates map images. When Summit displays a recorded route or makes a route share card, the requested map area can be based on the route. Summit draws the track itself over the map.
  • Open-Meteo. Forecast requests use the coordinates of the resort being checked.
  • OpenStreetMap via Overpass. When Summit fetches trail and lift data, it requests an area around the resort from overpass-api.de or its fallback, overpass.kumi.systems.
  • Terrain and land cover. Summit can request elevation tiles and ESA WorldCover files hosted on Amazon S3. Tile and file identifiers relate to the area needed for the resort map.

These online services receive the requests needed to provide their features and may receive connection information such as your IP address. Links to resort, road, pass, and other external sites open services governed by their own privacy practices.

iCloud and optional providers

iCloud sync is unavailable in the build covered by this policy. Summit has an opt-in sync feature for configured builds. If it becomes available and you turn it on, ski history can sync to your private iCloud database. The OnTheSnow and Utah DOT integrations require separate configuration and are inactive in the build covered here.

Imports, exports, and photos

Summit reads activity files you select to create ski days on your phone. You can export saved ski days from Profile › Export Data. JSON exports include GPS points and GPX exports include recorded routes. The destination you choose in the share sheet receives the file or image you share.

For trip posters, you can give Summit access to a photo by selecting it in the iOS photo picker. Summit requests add-only Photos permission when you choose to save a share image to your library.

Keeping and deleting your data

Saved ski days stay in your history until you delete them. Open a day in Ski History and select Delete Ski Day to remove it from Summit. Copies you exported, shared, or saved to Photos can be managed separately. You can change location access and Precise Location in your iPhone’s Settings.
